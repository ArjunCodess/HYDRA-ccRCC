\author{Arjun Vijay Prakash}

\begin{abstract}
Which tumor--normal transcriptomic associations in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) retain prognostic support under stronger validation? We used TCGA-KIRC for discovery and GSE40435 and GSE53757 for expression replication. Previously inspected GSE29609 and E-MTAB-1980 supplied external survival assessments. Expression replication, clinically adjusted survival models, and stricter sensitivity and effect gates reduced \MappedDeg\ discovery genes to \HighGenes\ priority associations. These \HighGenes\ retained conditional bootstrap support, but adding a gene selected within training folds changed held-out concordance by only \NestedDelta\ in \NestedPatients\ patients, with a conditional 95\% interval of \NestedCiLow\ to \NestedCiHigh. Survival direction agreed for \GseSame/\GseMapped\ mapped candidates in GSE29609 versus \EmSame/\EmMapped\ in E-MTAB-1980; two associations reversed with false-discovery-rate support in GSE29609. Marker-score adjustment removed support for \CompositionFailures\ associations, whereas consensus-purity adjustment retained all \HighGenes. The workflow exposes disagreement between expression reproducibility, prognostic association, and prediction. It supplies auditable evidence for follow-up, without establishing a transportable prognostic panel or clinical utility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A gene can distinguish a ccRCC tumor from normal kidney without improving prognosis beyond clinical stage and grade. HYDRA asks one primary question: \emph{which tumor--normal transcriptomic associations retain prognostic support as replication, clinical adjustment, held-out evaluation, and tissue-composition checks strengthen the evidence?} Three secondary questions ask whether survival associations agree across cohorts, whether the selection procedure improves prediction beyond clinical variables, and whether composition changes the interpretation of the associations.

The contribution is a reproducible workflow and a multi-cohort evidence audit. Familiar methods supply different tests of the same candidate claims, and a gene-level ledger preserves disagreements instead of turning a discovery shortlist into a validated panel. Differential expression, prognostic association, prediction performance, and biological plausibility are reported separately. Clinical utility is not evaluated. HYDRA introduces no statistical algorithm.

\subsection{Relation to biomarker-discovery practice}
REMARK asks investigators to report patient selection, covariates, assay methods, and validation clearly \cite{mcshane_remark_2005}. Selection-aware validation is also established: Simon and colleagues require feature selection to occur inside resampling when estimating a discovery procedure's performance \cite{simon_pitfalls_2003}. HYDRA applies these principles rather than proposing a new estimator. Its additional output is a linked record of how the same claims change under stronger evidence, including negative cohort results and competing composition explanations.

ClearCode34 developed a ccRCC subtype classifier and evaluated it in TCGA and independent clinical specimens \cite{brooks_clearcode34_2014}. HYDRA instead audits a tumor--normal-first prognostic procedure. Its signed ClearCode34 comparator is an adaptation for these splits, not a reproduction of the published classifier or a clinical head-to-head evaluation. The contribution beyond running familiar analyses sequentially is the explicit separation of selection gates from downstream assessments, a complete gene ledger, and comparisons of research procedures on common splits and matched list sizes. Whether this workflow produces better biomarkers remains an empirical question; the present comparisons do not establish general superiority.

\section{Evidence design}
TCGA-KIRC supplies \TumorPatients\ primary-tumor patients and \NormalPatients\ normal-kidney samples. One aliquot per patient is selected by library depth. The primary survival analysis includes \NestedPatients\ patients and \NestedEvents\ deaths after complete-case exclusions. GSE40435 and GSE53757 supply 101 and 72 tumor--normal pairs, respectively. GSE29609 contains \GsePatients\ patients and \GseEvents\ deaths; E-MTAB-1980 contains \EmPatients\ patients and \EmEvents\ deaths. These two survival cohorts were inspected before the current analysis and are exploratory checks, not untouched validation. Dataset identities and complete inclusion rules appear in the supplement and cohort dictionary.

The evidence funnel has six selection gates. TCGA differential expression requires false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.05 and absolute log$_2$ fold change at least one. Expression replication requires the same direction in both GEO cohorts and nominal support in at least one. Clinically adjusted Cox models then test continuous expression alongside age, sex, stage, and grade. Covariate sensitivities remove stage or grade separately. Two increasingly strict gates add effect-size and FDR thresholds. The final \HighGenes-gene set is a priority shortlist of associations, not a validated panel. Subsequent analyses assess this set without redefining it.

We distinguish four kinds of evidence. Cox coefficients describe prognostic association. Repeated held-out comparisons assess prediction performance beyond a clinical model. Purity and normal-tissue cell-source data inform biological interpretation. None evaluates clinical utility, which would require a defined clinical decision and prospective evidence. Treatment-effect modification is tested separately in the supplement.

For prediction, ten repeats of five patient folds rerun TCGA selection and training-only preprocessing. The GEO expression evidence remains fixed. Each fold adds its top qualifying gene to a clinical model. The project criterion requires mean concordance improvement of at least 0.01, a conditional patient-bootstrap interval above zero, and no worse three-year Brier score. The bootstrap resamples saved predictions and does not refit training models or redraw splits. Full-data coefficient bootstraps also condition on the selected shortlist. Model specifications, testing families, seeds, and secondary checks are in the supplement.

\section{Results along the evidence funnel}
\subsection{Discovery contracts to a small association shortlist}
The funnel contracts from \MappedDeg\ mapped TCGA discovery genes to \ReproDeg\ expression-reproducible genes, \MainSurvival\ adjusted survival associations, \SensitivityPass\ passing covariate sensitivity, \StrictGenes\ strict candidates, and \HighGenes\ priority associations. Figure~\ref{fig:funnel} shows the six selection gates and parallel downstream assessments; the counts are audited in the ledger. The ledger records each mapped gene, including failed gates and explicit missing downstream evidence, so favorable candidates cannot conceal the denominator.



Changing aliquot rules leaves \AliquotDrop, \AliquotFirst, or \AliquotSum\ priority genes instead of \HighGenes. This sensitivity shows that the shortlist boundary depends on sample handling. The unpaired GSE53757 analysis retains all \HighGenes\ genes, but agreement between fits cannot verify the assumed patient pairs. Full gene tables and both sensitivities are reported in the supplement.

\subsection{Survival evidence depends on the cohort}
E-MTAB-1980 retains the TCGA hazard direction for \EmSame\ of \EmMapped\ mapped genes; \EmStrict\ meet the directional FDR rule in both unadjusted and limited-adjustment models. GSE29609 retains the direction for only \GseSame\ of \GseMapped, with no same-direction nominal support. DDC and TCIRG1 have opposite-direction FDR support there. Cohort size, platform, and clinical adjustment differ, so these results describe disagreement rather than identifying its cause.

ACADM illustrates agreement between TCGA and E-MTAB-1980, while its GSE29609 estimate points in the opposite direction with uncertainty spanning no association. Figure~\ref{fig:replicated} shows every cohort rather than presenting the favorable comparison alone. The primary conclusion is therefore cohort-specific support, not uniform replication.

\subsection{Conditional association stability does not establish prediction}
All \HighGenes\ full-data selected genes have coefficient-bootstrap intervals excluding zero. That result addresses uncertainty after selection; it does not estimate stability of the selection procedure. Within the 50 training folds, the top gene varies across \NestedDistinctGenes\ genes, and the most frequent appears in \NestedTopGeneFolds\ folds. Adding the selected gene changes concordance by \NestedDelta\, with conditional 95\% interval \NestedCiLow\ to \NestedCiHigh, and three-year Brier score by \NestedBrierDelta. The procedure fails its project criterion: the mean concordance gain is below 0.01 and its interval spans zero. This does not establish that the underlying associations are null.

Existing procedure comparisons quantify how discovery choices change the evidence. At an equal requested list size of 22, DE-only selection versus the complete rule gives direction agreement of \ReviewEmDeSame/\ReviewEmDeMapped{}\ versus \ReviewEmCompleteSame/\ReviewEmCompleteMapped{} mapped genes in E-MTAB-1980, but \ReviewGseDeSame/\ReviewGseDeMapped{}\ versus \ReviewGseCompleteSame/\ReviewGseCompleteMapped{} in GSE29609. Same-direction FDR support is \ReviewEmDeFdr\ versus \ReviewEmCompleteFdr in E-MTAB-1980 and zero for both in GSE29609. The ranking rules and mapping denominators differ, and outcomes were previously inspected. These comparisons do not show that stricter filtering reliably improves transportability.

On identical nested TCGA splits, DE-only, survival-only, and HYDRA one-gene procedures change concordance by \BenchDeDelta, \BenchSurvivalDelta, and \BenchHydraDelta, respectively. Across those 50 folds, DE-only selection chooses \BenchDeGenes\ distinct genes versus \BenchHydraGenes\ for HYDRA, so stronger filtering does not make gene identity invariant. A separate ridge model using all training-fold reproducible genes changes concordance by \BenchRidgeDelta. Its Brier difference interval spans zero, and it has not been evaluated externally. This exploratory multigene comparison shows held-out ranking information within TCGA without rescuing the failed one-gene criterion or establishing a signature. Full uncertainty and comparator specifications are in the supplement.

\subsection{Composition changes the interpretation of some associations}
Consensus-purity adjustment on matched patients retains all \HighGenes\ hazard directions and FDR associations. Bulk marker-score adjustment removes FDR support for \CompositionFailures, leaving \ReviewMarkerRetained. DDC illustrates the tension: its GSE29609 association reverses, and marker adjustment attenuates its TCGA coefficient, whereas purity adjustment barely changes it. Figure~\ref{fig:contradiction} reports both results rather than choosing one composition measure as definitive.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
The strongest result is the mismatch between a reproducible association shortlist and the evidence needed for a transportable prognostic claim. Expression replication and covariate adjustment yield \HighGenes\ associations, yet cross-cohort directions disagree, the selected one-gene procedure adds little held-out discrimination, and some associations depend on the composition adjustment. The workflow makes these differences inspectable. It does not prove that evidence hardening is universally superior to simpler discovery procedures.

Several threats remain. Complete-case analysis excludes \NestedExcluded\ TCGA patients and does not model missingness. GSE53757 pair identities are inferred from adjacent sample order. TCGA differential expression has no explicit batch covariate, and the GEO analyses estimate zero surrogate variables, so unwanted variation and platform confounding remain possible. Distinct accessions do not establish patient independence: cross-accession overlap has not been verified using shared identities. The external cohorts differ in event counts and available clinical covariates, preventing an equivalent adjusted comparison.

Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) correction applies within defined testing families, not to every adaptive choice across the workflow. The expression screen and repeated project development limit interpretation of nominal FDR guarantees. \ReviewPhFlags\ primary gene models have nominal proportional-hazards flags; non-significant shape diagnostics cannot establish proportional hazards. Conditional bootstrap intervals omit selection and training uncertainty. Human Protein Atlas (HPA) describes normal-tissue cell sources, not ccRCC tumor cells. Previously inspected outcomes and bulk composition proxies cannot establish causality, prospective validation, or clinical utility. No decision threshold, prospective assay, or patient-benefit evaluation is available.

\section*{Data availability and reproducibility}
Public inputs are identified by accession and source URL, with a pinned TRACERx commit, cached-input hashes, package versions, and seeds. Original retrieval dates were not recorded and remain unknown. The revision records current execution dates separately from original data access. The candidate ledger and claim checks are generated by scripted commands; the source-to-output manifest identifies regenerated tables and figures. Cached data are excluded from Git and can be retrieved through the documented pipeline. Full procedures, gene tables, and secondary checks follow in the supplement.
