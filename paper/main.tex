\begin{abstract}
The one-gene HYDRA funnel fails its prespecified held-out test, while the broader reproducible gene set still carries ranking information. On one TCGA-KIRC tumor per patient, the selection-aware ten-repeat, five-fold analysis changed concordance by \NestedDelta\ (patient-bootstrap 95\% interval \NestedCiLow\ to \NestedCiHigh, conditional on the saved out-of-fold predictions). A training-only ridge Cox model of the reproducible genes on those same splits changed concordance by \BenchRidgeDelta\ (interval \BenchRidgeCiLow\ to \BenchRidgeCiHigh). The ridge penalty was chosen inside each training fold by partial-likelihood deviance; the median selected $\lambda$ was \RidgeLambdaMedian. That arm was not the acceptance test, it does not freeze a signature, and its three-year Brier interval still crossed zero. Survival-only and differential-expression-only single genes stayed within about 0.002 of the clinical concordance. The corrected funnel, from \OriginalTumorSamples\ aliquots to \TumorPatients\ patients, retained \ReproDeg\ reproducible differentially expressed genes, \StrictGenes\ strict candidates, and \HighGenes\ high-confidence candidates. A published 34-gene score on the same splits changed concordance by \ClearcodeDelta\ (interval \ClearcodeCiLow\ to \ClearcodeCiHigh). Alternate aliquot rules and an unpaired GSE53757 fit did not replace the primary shortlist. A matched-list comparison did not establish superiority over survival-only selection (GSE29609 directional-rate difference \GseFunnelDelta; 95\% bootstrap interval \GseFunnelCiLow\ to \GseFunnelCiHigh). E-MTAB-1980 gave strict same-direction support to \EmStrict\ of \EmMapped\ mapped candidates, while GSE29609 retained the TCGA direction for only \GseSame\ of \GseMapped\ and showed FDR-significant reversals for DDC and TCIRG1. No candidate-by-treatment interaction survived FDR correction in the CheckMate 025 RNA subset. These are candidate prognostic associations and audit findings, not a validated biomarker panel.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) has broad metabolic, vascular, and immune transcriptional changes \cite{tcga_kirc_2013,vhl_hif_review_2024}. A tumor--normal expression difference does not establish independent prognostic value: a signal can arise from the composition of the tissue, and repeated samples from one patient can make survival evidence appear more precise. We audited the public-data HYDRA-ccRCC funnel for these failure modes. The question is whether the one-gene rule generalizes, and whether the broader reproducible set still ranks patients when that rule does not.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Inputs, patient identity, and discovery}
TCGA-KIRC STAR unstranded counts and clinical outcomes were retrieved through TCGAbiolinks \cite{colaprico_tcgabiolinks_2016}. For each patient and sample type, we selected the aliquot with greatest raw-count library depth and broke barcode ties so the choice is deterministic rather than biological. The \OriginalTumorSamples\ tumor aliquots came from \TumorPatients\ patients, including \TcgaTumorEvents\ deaths: four patients contributed three aliquots each, which accounts for all eight extra aliquots. Of those \TumorPatients\ patients, \NestedExcluded\ are absent from the primary Cox model and the nested splits because each fails exactly one required field---\ExcludeGrade\ lack grade, \ExcludeStage\ lack stage, \ExcludeAge\ lacks age, and \ExcludeTime\ have overall-survival time recorded as zero---leaving \NestedPatients\ patients and \NestedEvents\ deaths (\ExcludeDeaths\ of the excluded patients died). The stage-only sensitivity restores the grade-missing patients (\StageOnlyPatients) and the grade-only sensitivity restores the stage-missing patients (\GradeOnlyPatients). All \NormalPatients\ normals were already unique by patient. Genes are matched by symbol after a dataset-specific collapse, recorded in the gene-mapping table. TCGA strips the Ensembl version, maps the id with org.Hs.eg.db, and keeps the first symbol. GSE40435, GSE53757, and GSE29609 take the first token of the platform gene-symbol field and average probes that share it. E-MTAB-1980 maps RefSeq identifiers the same way and averages rows that share a symbol. TRACERx matches candidate symbols to expression rownames. CheckMate 025 averages rows that share \texttt{gene\_name}. Survival-event coding was checked against vital status and the appropriate death or follow-up date; joins were required to remain one row per patient. Three alternate gates, reported as sensitivities, dropped those four patients, kept the first barcode, or summed a patient's tumor counts. Original retrieval dates for cached public inputs were unavailable, so the repository records source URLs and hashes without assigning new access dates.

DESeq2 compared the selected tumors and normals after requiring at least ten counts in ten samples \cite{love_deseq2_2014}. A separate patient-blocked DESeq2 fit, with design patient plus tissue type, used the \PairedPatients\ selected tumor--normal pairs. GSE40435 and GSE53757 were analyzed with patient-blocked limma; each parsed patient was required to have exactly one tumor and one normal, and surrogate-variable analysis protected the paired contrast \cite{ritchie_limma_2015,leek_sva_2012}. GSE53757 has no field that stores one shared patient id for every pair. Adjacent rows were opposite tissues and the same stage in all \PairSameStage\ pairs, and \PairSameToken\ pairs also shared a title token, so the primary pair ids follow that adjacent order. An unpaired limma fit is a sensitivity. A reproducible DEG required TCGA FDR below 0.05 and absolute log$_2$ fold change at least one, the same sign in both GEO cohorts, and nominal support in at least one, defined as a two-sided unadjusted $p<0.05$ with that same sign. A separate apeglm analysis tested all count-QC genes without the fold-change gate; it was not allowed to redefine the primary candidate set \cite{zhu_apeglm_2019}.

The primary Cox model used continuous standardized expression, age, sex, categorical AJCC stage with Stage I as the reference, and grade collapsed to G1+G2 versus G3+G4 among complete cases \cite{cox_1972}. G1 contains 14 patients in the TCGA clinical table, so it was not kept as its own level. The strict gate also required main-model FDR below 0.05, absolute log hazard ratio at least $\log(1.25)$, both GEO effects at least 0.25 in absolute log$_2$ units, and concordant nominal support in two sensitivity fits: expression plus age, sex, and stage, and expression plus age, sex, and grade. The high-confidence gate required FDR below 0.01 and absolute log hazard ratio at least $\log(1.5)$. Those magnitude cutoffs are prespecified project thresholds, not a cited minimal clinically important difference. False-discovery correction stays inside one family at a time: differential expression, each GEO cohort, Cox models among reproducible genes, and the high-confidence tests for shape, composition, and treatment interaction. Proportional-hazards tests were diagnostics, never selection gates. The selected gene is the one with the highest evidence score, $-{\log}_{10}(\mathrm{FDR}) + |\log \mathrm{HR}|$ plus bounded fold-change terms, with remaining ties broken by FDR and then gene id. The acceptance test adds that one gene. The ridge fit is a separate multigene description. It does not replace the failed one-gene criterion or freeze a signature. It is a glmnet Cox model with Efron ties, clinical covariates unpenalized, and an L2 penalty ($\alpha=0$) on every training-fold reproducible gene with nonzero training standard deviation. The penalty does not zero coefficients and does not name a gene subset. Before any penalty is chosen, the outer training fold fixes the design matrix: DESeq2 size factors are estimated on the training samples, test size factors use that training geometric-mean reference, expression is $\log_2(\mathrm{count}/\mathrm{size\ factor}+1)$, and genes with zero training standard deviation are dropped. Clinical factor levels come from the \NestedPatients-patient complete-case cohort; the values in a fit come from its training rows. $\lambda$ is then chosen by an inner cross-validation that uses only those training patients. \texttt{cv.glmnet} first fits the $\lambda$ path on all outer-training rows, scores mean partial-likelihood deviance on five event-stratified inner folds of those same rows, and keeps $\lambda_{\min}$. The criterion is deviance, not the concordance index and not the one-standard-error rule. Each inner fit standardizes its own training columns. The coefficients applied to the outer test rows come from the refit on the full outer training fold, and that fold's column means and standard deviations are applied to the test rows. Outer test outcomes are not an input. The inner-fold seed is the project seed plus $31000 + 10\times\mathrm{repeat} + \mathrm{fold}$, drawn before \texttt{cv.glmnet}. The reported contrast compares this joint fit with a separate clinical Cox model. Clinical coefficients inside the ridge fit are estimated together with the penalized genes, so the concordance increment is not an isolated gene effect and does not identify which genes produce the gain.

\subsection{Selection-aware prediction and null simulation}
Ten repeats of five event-stratified patient folds reran TCGA DE and every candidate gate within each training fold of the \NestedPatients\ complete-case patients defined above. Fold labels were redrawn for each repeat. Event stratification shuffled deaths and censored patients separately, and every outer test fold contained \FoldTestEvents\ deaths. The full-data stage-only and grade-only sensitivities contain \StageOnlyPatients\ and \GradeOnlyPatients\ patients, respectively, and are therefore not numerically identical to these nested fits. GEO expression evidence remained fixed; test patients and their normals were excluded from training. That is the scope of ``selection-aware'' here: TCGA differential expression, the fixed GEO gate, the Cox gates, and the choice of one gene. It does not re-split the GEO cohorts or re-choose the 0.01 rule inside a fold. Library-size normalization and expression scaling were estimated on training samples and applied to held-out tumors. The nested fit used log$_2$ normalized counts, while the full-data survival analysis used a variance-stabilizing transformation; this preserves training-only preprocessing but changes the expression scale, limiting direct coefficient comparisons. DESeq2 retained the primary outlier-replacement setting, with a logged no-replacement retry only if the pinned runtime failed in that step. The top qualifying gene was added to a clinical Cox model; when no gene qualified, its prediction equaled the clinical-only prediction. Out-of-fold concordance, three-year inverse-probability-of-censoring-weighted Brier score, and risk-score calibration slope were computed per repeat. The concordance increment received a patient-resampled 95\% interval conditional on the saved out-of-fold predictions; it does not include variation from refitting selection or redrawing folds. The prespecified success rule required an increment at least 0.01, a conditional interval above zero, and a mean Brier difference no greater than zero. The 0.01 increment is this project's size requirement, not a universal clinical standard. A lower Brier score is not, by itself, better calibration: discrimination and calibration contribute separately to the Brier score.

Two hundred clinical-only null datasets used an exponential baseline calibrated to the clinical Cox cumulative hazard with resampled censoring; each reran the full training selection on one held-out fold, cycling across the five fixed folds from repeat 1. This is a parametric clinical-risk null, not another ten-by-five-fold interval and not a p-value for the concordance increment. A second null permuted training survival pairs on the five saved repeat-1 differential-expression fits, without refitting DESeq2, and cycled the same way across 200 simulations. The original 1,000 patient bootstraps and 20-repeat per-gene CV condition on the full-data shortlist and remain descriptive rather than selection-adjusted evidence.

The same patients, seeds, and event-stratified folds were then used to compare selectors. One training-only DESeq2 fit supplied six predictors for each held-out fold: the clinical model; the primary HYDRA gene; a survival-only gene held to the same high-confidence hazard gates without tumor--normal or GEO filters; a differential-expression-only gene held to the training DESeq2 thresholds without survival or GEO filters; a ridge Cox model on every training-fold reproducible DEG, with clinical covariates unpenalized and $\lambda_{\min}$ chosen by the training-only inner deviance cross-validation above \cite{simon_glmnet_cox_2011}; and one expression-matched gene at or below the median residual correlation with the clinical martingale residuals. Survival-only false-discovery rates were computed as if every unscreened count-QC gene had $p=1$, after fitting the top 2,000 residual correlations. The HYDRA gene was required to match the saved primary nested result. Intervals remain conditional on the saved predictions. This comparison does not replace the prespecified one-gene acceptance rule.

On those same folds we also added ClearCode34, the published 34-gene ccA/ccB score \cite{brooks_clearcode34_2014}. ccA genes were signed negative and ccB genes positive. Current symbols were taken from gene aliases where the published symbol has changed; one published symbol did not map. Expression was log$_2$ counts per million, and the training fold supplied the mean and standard deviation. This arm does not select genes. Separately, on the previously inspected E-MTAB-1980 cohort, we fit in-sample Cox models for the study's ccA/ccB label, for the highest-evidence primary gene present on that array (CRYL1; CYFIP2 did not map), and for a sign-weighted mean of the mapped high-confidence genes, each added to the limited clinical model. Those E-MTAB fits are not nested, and their in-sample calibration slopes are not held-out estimates.

\subsection{Exploratory alternatives and biological checks}
At the largest common list size of 22 genes, we compared the complete rule with survival-only, DE-only, each leave-one-GEO-out rule, and TCGA-expression-matched non-prognostic controls. GSE29609 and E-MTAB-1980 were previously inspected, so both evaluations are exploratory. We estimated the complete-rule minus survival-only directional-rate difference and a 2,000-draw paired gene bootstrap in each cohort. The prespecified funnel criterion required an interval entirely above zero.

Candidate Cox models were refit on matched complete cases with published TCGA consensus tumor purity \cite{aran_purity_2015} or leave-one-candidate-out marker scores. Each score is the mean of sample-standardized marker expression, then standardized across samples. The sets are proximal tubule (AQP1, LRP2, CUBN, SLC5A2, ALDOB), endothelial (PECAM1, VWF, KDR, EMCN, TEK), immune (PTPRC, CD3D, CD8A, MS4A1, CD68), and stromal (COL1A1, COL1A2, DCN, LUM, ACTA2). A candidate that belongs to a set is omitted from that score. These are bulk marker scores, not measured cell proportions. Consensus purity is an inferred bulk estimate. Nonlinear expression and expression-by-log-time models were tested against the linear proportional-hazards fit. Human Protein Atlas normal-tissue single-cell profiles informed cell-source cautions but were not treated as ccRCC tumor single-cell validation.

TRACERx Renal provided primary-region expression and linked survival data from a pinned public repository \cite{fernandez_sanroman_tracerx_2025}. One thousand repeats selected one region per patient. A fixed event-stratified 39-patient subset isolated region-choice variation; a separate scenario resampled which 39 patients entered each repeat. CheckMate 025 supplementary RNA data supported post hoc continuous-expression-by-treatment Cox interactions for overall and progression-free survival, with FDR correction within endpoint and model \cite{braun_checkmate_2020}.

\subsection{Spatial sampling and randomized treatment}

A fixed event-stratified subset of \TracerxSubsetPatients\ patients matches the size of GSE29609 and isolates region choice. Across genes, median agreement with the TCGA hazard direction was \TracerxFixedMedian\% (interquartile range \TracerxFixedIqrLow\ to \TracerxFixedIqrHigh) when those patients were fixed and only the chosen region varied, versus \TracerxChangingMedian\% (interquartile range \TracerxChangingIqrLow\ to \TracerxChangingIqrHigh) when both patient membership and region varied. Each smaller subset had \TracerxSubsetEvents\ deaths. That event count makes this a sampling-variation check. It cannot attribute GSE29609's reversals to spatial sampling alone.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
The funnel fails as a one-gene selection procedure. The broader reproducible transcriptomic space still contains held-out prognostic ranking information. Counting each patient once, then repeating differential expression and candidate selection inside held-out folds, changed concordance by \NestedDelta, and the conditional patient-bootstrap interval crossed zero. That is a failure of the prediction criterion. It is not evidence that the \HighGenes\ associations are null, and it is not a deployed signature. The ridge concordance increment of \BenchRidgeDelta\ cleared 0.01 on the same splits. The penalty was training-only, the Brier interval still crossed zero, and the comparison was specified after the one-gene rule, so it does not replace the failed one-gene criterion or freeze a signature. The TRACERx median of \TracerxFixedMedian\% is not near-complete agreement: the interquartile range across genes runs from \TracerxFixedIqrLow\% to \TracerxFixedIqrHigh\% even when the \TracerxSubsetPatients\ patients stay fixed. With \TracerxSubsetEvents\ deaths, those draws cannot explain the GSE29609 reversals. Marker-score and consensus-purity adjustment also disagree, so a composition result from one of them is not a composition result from both.

Correcting patient identity changed the shortlist from 27 to \HighGenes\ genes. The four genes that leave under every alternate aliquot rule show that the edge of the high-confidence set is sensitive to how the eight extra aliquots are handled. The unpaired GEO gate did not remove the 23. The permutation null selected a gene in \PermSelected\ of 200 simulations, so scrambled training survival did not pass the high-confidence gate. That count is not a test of the concordance increment. ClearCode34's held-out increment was larger than the one-gene funnel increment, and its conditional interval still included zero and values below 0.01. Conditional coefficient precision and single-gene holdout performance did not translate into a prespecified gain for the complete training-selected procedure: its mean concordance increment was below 0.01, its conditional patient-bootstrap interval crossed zero, and gene identity varied between training folds. This is a failure of the stated prediction criterion, not evidence that every selected association is null. The same-split comparison locates that failure more narrowly. Removing the tumor--normal gate or the survival gate, and adding only the resulting gene, did not improve held-out ranking. Keeping every training-fold reproducible gene in a ridge model did. The concordance gain cleared 0.01 with an interval above zero, while the Brier interval still included a worse score and the calibration slope stayed near the clinical value. The eligible expression set is therefore not empty of ranking information. The one-gene rule did not carry that ranking gain into held-out patients. The ridge fit is still not a locked signature: the penalty and the gene set were re-estimated in each training fold, GEO evidence stayed fixed, and the patient bootstrap did not refit that selection or the penalty. The increment also mixes penalized expression with clinical coefficients re-estimated in the same fit. No offset model separates those two pieces, so the analysis does not say which genes produce the ranking gain and does not support a clinical action. The unpaired GSE53757 check kept the high-confidence genes. It does not establish that the adjacent-row identifiers are true patient pairs. The matched-list external comparison likewise failed to show a directional-replication advantage over survival-only ranking. None of these results supports a deployed multigene panel.

This is a retrospective public-data analysis. GSE29609 has only 17 deaths, E-MTAB-1980 has 23, and both were inspected before this reanalysis; no untouched external patient cohort is available. The matched-list interval resamples genes even though co-expression can make their directional results dependent, so it is descriptive rather than a confirmatory confidence interval. The 39-patient TRACERx subsets have nine deaths and do not recreate GSE29609's platform or case mix. Consensus purity and bulk marker scores are imperfect composition measures; HPA profiles normal tissue, and tumor single-cell, protein, and functional validation are unavailable. The post hoc CheckMate 025 RNA subset and everolimus comparator do not validate predictive effects in current treatment settings. RNA associations cannot establish a mechanism or therapeutic target.
